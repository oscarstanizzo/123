The order of work

A body passes through
four rooms, in this order.

  1. 01

    The mill room

    Two five-axis machines, and the only room with a door that closes. A body starts as a bar of Grade 5 and takes eleven hours, eleven passes, and about eight per cent of the bar with it.

    • 11 h per body
    • 2 µm final pass
    • 8% of the bar survives
  2. 02

    The lapping bench

    Nine seats, though rarely nine people. Chamfers are lapped against a cotton wheel by hand — forty minutes a body, and the only part of the process that has never been made faster.

    • 40 min per body
    • 9 cleared hands
    • 0 machines
  3. 03

    The sapphire room

    Kept at nineteen degrees and unpleasantly clean. Crystals arrive grown and cut; they are seated, coated on both faces, and checked against a light box by eye before anything is bonded.

    • 2.1 mm crystal
    • 19 °C held
    • Both faces coated
  4. 04

    The ledger room

    Where a phone becomes a serial. Colour is calibrated against a physical chart, the inside of the body is signed, and one line is written in the book by the door. Then it is boxed in ash.

    • 1 line per phone
    • Signed inside
    • Ash box

The nine

Everyone allowed to touch
a chamfer, and since when.

Clearance is not a job title. It means a finisher has lapped enough bodies, under enough supervision, that theirs go out unchecked. The shortest anyone has taken to get there is two years and one month.

The book by the door

Every phone we have
made has a line.

Series, serial, the date it was finished, and the initials of whoever lapped it. It is how service works ten years later: you give us a serial, we find the line, and the person who finished it is usually still here.

Seven series, nine thousand four hundred and eleven lines, and one crossed out — a body dropped down the goods lift in 2023.
