The house  —  how we started

Two people, a drawer of tools, and a box marked the argument.

Pear was not founded to disrupt anything. It was founded because a woman who finished watch movements for nineteen years was made redundant, and a man who packaged semiconductors kept a box of phones that had died for stupid reasons. They met over a broken thing. This is the whole story.

Ilse Brandt

Nineteen years bevelling edges
that nobody would ever see.

Ilse finished movements — anglage, the hand-polished bevel along the inside edge of a bridge. It is done under a loupe, with a boxwood peg, on a surface the owner of the watch will never look at. Her house did it because the rule was that the inside is finished like the outside.

In 2016 the house closed its finishing department. She was given a letter, three weeks, and the drawer of tools she had ground to her own hand over two decades. She kept the drawer.

Tomás Refai

Forty-one dead phones,
none of them dead for a good reason.

Tomás spent six years in semiconductor packaging, which is the unglamorous business of connecting a very good chip to the rest of a product. He was good at it and it bored him, because the chip was almost never the thing that failed.

He kept the ones that came back. A battery swollen inside a case glued shut. A screen bonded to the frame so the repair cost more than the phone. A camera module discontinued eleven months after launch. Forty-one of them, in a box he labelled, without much humour, the argument.

  3. 2018

    A repair café on Rue du Seyon

    They meet on a Saturday. Ilse is opening a phone with watchmaker's tools because they are the only ones small enough. Tomás watches her do it better than he can. The bet is made that afternoon: one phone, made the way a movement is made.

  4. 2019

    Fourteen cracked bodies

    Titanium work-hardens as you cut it. Push the last pass too fast and the chamfer crazes days later, in a drawer, quietly. Fourteen bodies failed before they understood why.

    14 scrapped
  5. 2021

    Two microns, and the lease

    The fifteenth body held. The difference was a final pass of two microns, which is slow enough to be uneconomic and is still how every Ora is cut. They take the lease at Rue du Seyon 14 — the café.

2019 – 2021

The part where it
did not work.

A cast body would have worked immediately. Milling from solid is harder for one reason that nobody warns you about: titanium work-hardens under the tool, so the last pass — the one that makes the edge — is the pass most likely to ruin it. The cracks did not appear on the machine. They appeared days later, in a drawer.

Fourteen bodies went in the bin over fourteen months. The fix was not clever. It was slower.
